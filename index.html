      General Information

      Cancerous (Malignant) Tumors

      • Can grow quickly and invade nearby tissue.
      • May spread to other body areas (metastasis), depending on type.
      • Usually need urgent specialist evaluation and treatment planning.

      Non-cancerous (Benign) Tumors

      • Do not spread to distant organs.
      • Can still cause serious symptoms by pressing on brain or spinal structures.
      • Management may include monitoring, surgery, or targeted therapy.

      Brain Tumors

      • Common symptoms: headaches, seizures, memory/speech changes, weakness, vision changes.
      • Diagnosis often uses MRI/CT and sometimes biopsy.
      • Treatment can include surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, and rehabilitation.

      Spinal Tumors

      • Can occur in spinal cord, surrounding membranes, or vertebrae.
      • Common symptoms: back pain, numbness, weakness, walking difficulty, bladder/bowel changes.
      • Early diagnosis helps reduce risk of long-term neurologic damage.
